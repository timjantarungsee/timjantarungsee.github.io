Mlabri is my favourite language spoken by my favourite people. 

Considered by some (maybe sensationalist) sources to be "The last hunter-gatherers in Mainland South-East Asia", the Mlabri are a semi-nomadic ethno-linguistic group living in scattered settlements across rural Northern Thailand. There is no good estimate on the population, though most sources land anywhere between 150-400 people. They blend traditional hunter-gathering and nomadic lifestyle strategies with engaging in trade and working for other minority ethnic groups as farm labourers.

I naively sought them out when I was a masters student, and have since routinely visited to camp out with them to learn their language and elicit data for research. There isn't a lot of stuff out there regarding their language, so my ultimate goal is to create accessible resources about their language for them to keep. 

The Mlabri are undergoing a humanitarian crisis. Deforestation has led to less resources in the forest to hunt and gather leading to widespread poverty which fuels an array of other social issues. Right now, there is a shortage of food and general necessities, and ongoing long-term issues related to the lack of land and exploitation. Every year, Christopher Davis and I raise money to personally help with short term issues (food and supply shortages), and discuss with the community how to improve their well-being in the long-term.
