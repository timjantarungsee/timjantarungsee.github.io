Considered by some (maybe sensationalist) sources to be "The last hunter-gatherers in Mainland South-East Asia", the Mlabri are a semi-nomadic ethno-linguistic group living in scattered settlements across rural Northern Thailand. There is no good estimate on the population, though most sources land anywhere between 150-400 people. They blend traditional hunter-gathering and nomadic lifestyle strategies with engaging in trade and seasonal work for other minority ethnic groups as farm labourers.

I naively sought them out when I was a masters student, and have since routinely visited to camp out with them, learn their language, and elicit data for research. There isn't a lot of stuff out there regarding their language, so I've been preparing a grammar from the ground up. I am also equally interested in anthropological research.

The Mlabri are undergoing a humanitarian crisis. Deforestation has led to less resources in the forest leading to widespread poverty. There is an immediate crisis concerning the lack of food and general necessities, and an on-going crisis related to the lack of land and labour exploitation. Every year, Christopher Davis and I raise money to personally help with short term issues (food and supply shortages), and discuss with the community how to improve their well-being in the long-term.
