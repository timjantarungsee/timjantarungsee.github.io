Considered by some sensationalist sources to be "The last hunter-gatherers in Mainland South-East Asia", the Mlabri are an ethno-linguistic group living in scattered settlements across rural Northern Thailand. There is no good estimate on the population, though most sources land anywhere between 150-400 people. They blend traditional hunter-gathering and nomadic lifestyle strategies with working for other minoritiy ethnic groups in farms. 

I naively sought them out when I was a masters student, and have since routinely visited to live how they live, learn their language, and elicit data for research. With respect to their language, there exists scant sketches and wordlists, with basically nothing else.  

The Mlabri are undergoing a humanitarian crisis due to several factors. Deforestation has led to less resources in the forest to hunt and gather. Recent integration into `modern' Thai society as an isolated group is largely unregulated, leading to widespread poverty which fuels other social issues. Every year, Christopher Davis and I raise money to personally help with short term issues (food and supply shortages), and discuss with the community how to improve their well-being in the long-term.
